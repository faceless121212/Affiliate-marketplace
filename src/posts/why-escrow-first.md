Picture this. You spend three weekends building a review page for a new program. Traffic comes. Conversions come. The dashboard says you're owed a nice sum.

Then the payout date passes. Then another. Then the support emails stop getting answers.

If that's happened to you, you already know what's broken about affiliate marketing. If it hasn't, lucky you. Here's why we built Nativness the other way around.

> **The short version.** In most programs you work first and get paid later, if the advertiser is still willing and able. On Nativness the advertiser locks the commission budget BEFORE the offer is visible to anyone.

[[toc]]

## The problem: you carry all the risk

In a typical program, the affiliate spends first. You buy the traffic, write the content and build the creative. Payment comes later, on the network's schedule.

And here's the unfair part. The advertiser knows whether the budget exists. You don't.

A commission rate on a page is a promise. A promise costs nothing to make.

## 3 reasons we lock the budget first

### 1. You can see the money before you start

On Nativness, an offer doesn't appear until its commission budget is locked. The offer page shows the balance that's still there. Not a rate and a hope.

### 2. You can count what's left

Every offer shows how many payouts the remaining balance can still fund. If it's nearly empty, you'll see that too, and you can decide not to spend a weekend on it.

### 3. The advertiser has skin in the game

Anyone can list a program with a generous rate. Putting the budget down first is different. It tells you the advertiser is serious before you do any work.

## What escrow doesn't fix

Let's be honest about the limits.

A locked budget tells you an offer CAN pay. It doesn't tell you whether your audience will buy. It doesn't settle what counts as a conversion, either. That's what the offer's terms are for, so read them.

## Final thoughts

Escrow isn't magic. It just moves one risk from your side of the table to the advertiser's.

That feels like the right way round. They know if the money exists. Now you do too.
