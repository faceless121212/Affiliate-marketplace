You've found an offer you like. Good commission, product you'd actually recommend.

Before you write a word about it, look at three numbers. Together they answer the only question that matters at this stage: can this offer still pay me?

It takes about ten seconds.

[[toc]]

## The 3 numbers

### 1. Remaining, not deposited

The big figure on every offer is what's STILL locked. Money already paid out to other affiliates isn't counted.

**Why it matters:** an offer that deposited a large budget months ago might have very little left today. The deposit tells you about the past. The remaining balance tells you about your payout.

### 2. Commission per conversion

This is what one confirmed conversion pays. On its own it's just a rate. Next to the balance, it becomes useful.

### 3. Payouts it can still fund

Divide the remaining balance by the commission. That's how many conversions the offer can pay for right now.

**Quick example:** $340 left at $24 per conversion funds 14 more payouts. A high commission with a small balance might only fund a handful.

## What the colors mean

You don't have to do the math every time. The meter does it for you.

- **Lime:** funded. Plenty left.
- **Orange:** nearly spent. The balance has dropped to a tenth of its deposit.
- **"Cannot pay out":** the balance can't cover one more conversion, so the offer stops handing out links.

## 2 quick tips

### Check before every campaign, not just the first

Balances fall as other affiliates get paid. Top-ups are the advertiser's choice. So a healthy offer last month might be nearly spent today.

### Match your effort to the balance

A balance that funds five payouts doesn't justify a month of content. One that funds forty might. Let the number set your ambition.

## Final thoughts

Three numbers, ten seconds, and you know whether an offer can pay.

It's the check most programs never let you make. Use it every time.
