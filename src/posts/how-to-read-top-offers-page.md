You open a marketplace's "top offers this month" page. Next to one offer it says something like **EPC $3.00**.

Three dollars a click? You start doing the math on your traffic. A thousand clicks a month, that's...

Stop there. That number isn't a promise. It isn't even about you. Let's unpack what these pages actually say, and what they quietly leave out.

> **The short version.** EPC, APV, AOV and hop conversion are averages from other people's traffic. The two monthly pages we checked don't say how offers get on the list or which dates the numbers cover. Treat them as a list of questions, not a forecast.

## The four numbers, in plain English

| You see | It means | Think of it as |
|---|---|---|
| **EPC** | Earnings per click | What one click was worth, on average |
| **APV** | Average payout value | Your cut of one sale |
| **AOV** | Average order value | What the customer spent |
| **Hop conversion** | Clicks that became a first sale | How well the sales page closes |

They're all connected. Here's a made-up example, so you can see the gears turn.

Say a course sells for **$200**. That's the AOV. The program pays **40%**, so your cut is **$80**. That's the APV. If **2 out of every 100 clicks** buy, each click was worth **$1.60**. That's the EPC.

Change any one of those and the EPC moves. Which brings us to the catches.

## Catch 1: it's someone else's traffic

EPC blends every affiliate's clicks into one number. A warm email list and a cold ad audience land in the same average.

So a high EPC might mean a great offer. Or it might mean a few affiliates with very loyal audiences. The page can't tell you which.

## Catch 2: "earnings" isn't always your earnings

This one surprised us. ClickBank's own pages define EPC two ways. Its glossary says "Revenue / total clicks". Its help center says it's the "net commission earned per click".

Revenue is what the customer paid. Commission is what you keep. Those are very different numbers, and the monthly page doesn't say which one it's showing.

## Catch 3: APV counts money you haven't earned yet

On ClickBank, APV is another name for "Avg $ per conversion". The help center says that figure includes **initial sales, upsells and recurring rebills**.

So an $80 APV doesn't mean $80 lands on day one. Part of it might arrive over the following months, and only if the customer keeps paying.

**Ask for the first-sale commission on its own.** That's the number you can count on.

## Catch 4: AOV without a commission rate tells you nothing

ClickBank's monthly page prints APV. Digistore24's prints AOV. For most of the offers on Digistore24's September list, the commission field said "Upon Request".

An AOV of $200 at 40% pays you $80. At 25% it pays $50. Same AOV, very different month.

## Catch 5: nobody says how the list was made

This is the big one. We looked for a ranking rule on both pages and didn't find one.

- **ClickBank** calls its list the "highest-performing" products "right now".
- **Digistore24** says "we handpick" its offers.

Neither page names the metric, the cut-off or the dates. A page titled "September" could be showing 30 days of data, or 90, or something else.

ClickBank's help center does give windows for its marketplace stats, and they don't match each other: **30 days** for EPC and conversion rate, **90 days** for average commission. So even on one platform, the numbers beside an offer may come from different stretches of time.

## So is the page useless?

No. It's a good shortlist. It's just a bad forecast.

Use it to find offers worth a closer look. Then ask the seller five things:

1. **Is the EPC based on commission or revenue?** And over which dates?
2. **What's the first-sale commission**, without rebills and upsells?
3. **Where does the traffic come from?** Email, paid or search?
4. **What's the refund rate?** Neither monthly page shows one.
5. **What's the commission rate**, if it says "Upon Request"?

Then run a small test with your own traffic. Your EPC is the only one that matters.

## Quick questions

**Is a higher EPC always better?**
No. An offer with a lower EPC can earn you more if it fits your audience. A cooking blog won't convert a software offer, whatever the page says.

**Is EPC per click or per 100 clicks?**
On the ClickBank and Digistore24 pages we read, it's per click. Some networks are said to quote it per 100 clicks. We couldn't confirm that on their own pages, so check the definition wherever you see the number.

**Why is APV higher than the commission on the offer page?**
Because it can include upsells and rebills on top of the first sale.

**Can I compare an offer on one marketplace with one on another?**
Only roughly. They print different numbers over unstated periods.
