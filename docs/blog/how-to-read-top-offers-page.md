# EPC, APV, AOV and hop conversion: how to read a marketplace's "top offers this month" page

By the Nativness team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated October 3, 2026 · 7 min read

*Nativness publishes this page. Nativness is a devnet prototype with simulated escrow and no live offers or payout data, nothing here is ranked by performance, and we earn nothing from the links on this page.*

A "top offers this month" page prints three or four averages beside each offer. EPC is earnings per click, APV is the average commission per sale, AOV is the average order size, and hop conversion is the share of clicks that buy. The two pages we checked print these numbers without saying how the offers were ranked or which dates the numbers cover.

## Key takeaways

- EPC, APV and hop conversion describe one relationship: commission per sale times the share of clicks that buy gives earnings per click.
- AOV is what the customer spends and APV is what you are paid, so the two are not comparable across pages without the commission rate.
- Neither the ClickBank nor the Digistore24 monthly page states its ranking metric or the dates its numbers cover.
- ClickBank's help center gives windows for its marketplace stats: 30 days for EPC and conversion rate, 90 days for average commission per conversion.
- Every figure is an average across other affiliates' traffic, so treat it as a starting question and not as a forecast for yours.

## How we chose and checked

We looked at the two marketplaces that publish a monthly offers page on their own blog: ClickBank and Digistore24. Both pages were dated September 25, 2026 when we opened them. For each printed metric we then looked for the company's own definition in its help center, glossary or blog.

We considered eight pages for definitions and used six. We excluded two network help pages that were blocked when we tried to open them, and every third-party glossary, because they disagree with each other. We name no offers from either list, so the examples below use made-up numbers.

## What does EPC tell you, and what does it hide?

EPC tells you the average earned per click sent to an offer. It hides everything about where those clicks came from.

ClickBank's guide gives the arithmetic: earnings of $1,000 across 2,000 clicks is an EPC of $0.50. The definitions still differ in one word. ClickBank's glossary writes EPC as "Revenue / total clicks", while its help center says "net commission earned per click". Digistore24's definition, written for vendors, says "revenue". Revenue and commission are different amounts, and the monthly pages do not say which one their EPC figure uses.

EPC also blends every affiliate's traffic into one number, and the page shows you only the mix.

## What is APV, and is it the same as "Avg $ per conversion"?

APV is the average commission paid per sale, and ClickBank treats it as another name for its marketplace stat. Its guide says "Avg $/Conversion" is "sometimes called average payout value, or APV".

The help center adds two details that the monthly page omits. The stat covers "the last 90 days", and it "includes all commission types - initial sales, upsells, and recurring rebills". So an APV can be higher than the commission on the first sale, and part of it arrives only if the customer stays.

## Why does one page print AOV and the other APV?

They measure different sides of the same sale. AOV is the customer's order and APV is your share of it.

The ClickBank page prints APV. The Digistore24 page prints AOV, and for most of its September offers the commission field reads "Upon Request". Without the rate you cannot turn an AOV into a payout.

Here is the conversion with made-up numbers. A course sells for $200, so AOV is $200. At a 40 percent revenue share, APV is $80. At 25 percent it is $50. The same AOV supports both.

## What does hop conversion measure?

Hop conversion is the share of clicks on affiliate links that become a first sale. ClickBank calls its affiliate link a "HopLink", which is where the name comes from.

The help center defines the marketplace version as the "average rate at which clicks from an affiliate link resulted in an initial sale in the last 30 days". Note "initial sale". Rebills do not count here, although they do count in APV.

The three numbers connect. With the made-up course above, an $80 APV and 2 clicks in 100 buying gives $1.60 per click. On a real page the product will rarely match exactly, because ClickBank's help center measures conversion and EPC over 30 days and average commission over 90.

## How are the offers on these pages ranked?

Neither page says. ClickBank describes its list as the "highest-performing" products on the marketplace "right now" and says it is updated every month. Digistore24 says "we handpick" the offers.

Neither names the metric used, the cut-off, or whether editors can add or remove an offer. ClickBank lists five offers and one bonus for September. Digistore24 lists five.

ClickBank does publish one popularity signal elsewhere. Gravity reflects "how many affiliates have earned a commission by promoting this offer". Its help center gives the window as "the past 90 days", and a ClickBank blog post dated February 20, 2026 describes "a 12-week rolling period". The monthly page does not say whether gravity plays any part in the list.

## What time period do the numbers cover?

The monthly pages do not state one. A page titled for September could show September figures, a trailing 30 days or a trailing 90 days.

This matters most for seasonal and new offers, where one strong week can lift a short average. If the window is not printed, ask before you plan spend around the figure.

## How should you use a top offers page?

Use it as a list of questions for the seller. For each offer you are considering, ask:

1. Is the EPC based on commission or on revenue, and over which dates?
2. Does the APV include rebills and upsells, and what is the first-sale commission alone?
3. What share of traffic behind the conversion rate is email, paid or organic?
4. What is the refund or cancellation rate? Neither monthly page prints one.
5. What is the commission rate, where the page says "Upon Request"?

Then test with a little of your own traffic and compare.

## FAQ

**Is a higher EPC always better?**
No. EPC is an average over other affiliates' traffic and, on these pages, over an unstated period. An offer with a lower printed EPC can earn more for you if it fits your audience.

**Is EPC quoted per click or per 100 clicks?**
The ClickBank and Digistore24 definitions we opened are per click. Some networks are said to quote per 100 clicks. We could not open their own pages to confirm it, so check the definition wherever you see the figure.

**Why is APV higher than the first-sale commission I see on the offer?**
ClickBank's average commission per conversion includes upsells and recurring rebills as well as the initial sale. The first sale alone can pay less than the printed average.

**Can I compare an offer on ClickBank with one on Digistore24?**
Only roughly. One monthly page prints APV and hop conversion, the other prints AOV, and neither states its data window.
