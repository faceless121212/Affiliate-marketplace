# USDC or USDT: which should an affiliate ask to be paid in?

By the Nativness team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated October 3, 2026 · 7.5 min read

*Nativness publishes this page. Nativness is a devnet prototype with simulated escrow and no live offers or payout data, nothing here is ranked by performance, and we earn nothing from the links on this page.*

Ask for the one you can turn into your own currency where you live, at a service you already use. Neither issuer will redeem small amounts for an individual directly, so the exchange or payment app you cash out through matters more than the token. If you live in the EEA, check which token your provider still supports before you agree.

## Key takeaways

- You will almost certainly cash out through an exchange or app, not with the issuer, because Circle requires a Circle Mint account and Tether sets a 100,000 USD minimum.
- Both issuers reserve the right to freeze tokens at an address, so neither is beyond an issuer's reach.
- Circle publishes a MiCA white paper naming a French electronic money institution as the EU issuer of USDC. We found no equivalent document on the Tether pages we opened.
- On Solana each token has one issuer-published mint address, and your wallet needs a separate token account for each.
- The network fee to receive either token on Solana is the same, because the fee is charged per signature and not per token.

## Can you redeem either token for dollars yourself?

In practice, no. Both issuers redeem only for their own verified customers, and the terms are written for businesses.

Circle's USDC Terms say: "You may not redeem USDC with Circle unless and until you open a Circle Mint account." Circle's site describes Circle Mint as a service for institutions and says individuals reach USDC through exchanges and wallets.

Tether's fee page sets the minimum acquisition or redemption at 100,000 USD. The redemption fee is "the greater of $1,000 or 0.1%", and there is a verification fee of 150 USD in Tether Tokens. Its terms also bar "U.S. Persons" from redeeming, apart from an exception Tether grants at its own discretion.

So the 1:1 promise is one you rely on indirectly. What you actually get is the price your exchange or app gives you on the day, minus its fees.

## Which one can you cash out where you live?

This is the question that should decide it. Open the service you plan to use, look for a withdrawal to your bank in your currency, and check which token and which network it accepts.

Check the network as carefully as the token. Circle says USDC is natively issued on 38 blockchains as of September 16, 2026, and Tether lists 14. A provider that accepts USDT on one network may not accept it on Solana. Sending a token over a network your provider does not support can leave the funds out of reach.

Ask the program which network it pays on before you give it an address.

## What changes if you live in the EU or EEA?

The legal position of the two tokens is documented differently, and that affects which services can offer them to you.

Circle publishes a MiCA white paper for USDC. It states that "USDC is defined as an e-money token pursuant to Article 3.1(7) of MiCA" and that the EU issuer is a licensed electronic money institution in France. It also says: "The holders of this e-money token have a right of redemption at any time and at par value. This applies to holders of USDC resident in the EEA."

We did not find a MiCA white paper or an EU authorization statement for USDT on the Tether pages we opened. That is a statement about those pages, not a legal conclusion. Your provider's own notice to EEA customers is the document that tells you what it supports.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

## Can the issuer freeze what you were paid?

Yes, with either token. Both sets of terms reserve that power.

Circle "reserves the right to 'block' certain USDC addresses and freeze associated USDC that it determines may be associated with illegal activity". Tether's terms say it may "freeze any Tether Tokens held by you" as required by law "or where Tether, in its sole discretion, determines it is prudent to do so".

For an affiliate this is mostly a counterparty question. Tokens that arrive from an address later linked to illegal activity can become a problem for you too. Know who is paying you, and keep the record of what the payment was for.

## What does it cost to receive either one on Solana?

The network fee is the same for both tokens, and the sender pays it. Solana's documentation gives a base fee of 5,000 lamports per signature, plus an optional prioritization fee.

There is one cost that can surprise a first-time recipient. Solana's docs say: "A wallet needs a token account for each token (mint) it wants to hold." Creating that account "requires a refundable minimum balance for its account storage", and whoever sends the instruction funds it. If you have never held the token, ask whether the payer creates the account or expects you to.

## How do you make sure you are receiving the real token?

Compare the mint address with the one the issuer publishes. Anyone can create a Solana token and name it "USDC" or "USDT".

Circle lists the Solana address for USDC as `EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v`. Tether lists USDT on Solana as `Es9vMFrzaCERmJfrF4H2FYD4KCoNkY11McCe8BenwNYB`. Copy the address from the issuer's page on the day, not from this article or a message someone sent you.

Bridged versions exist as well. Circle's address page warns that one network carries a bridged token labeled `USDC.e` "which is not issued or backed by Circle". If a program pays a bridged token, the issuer's terms do not cover it.

## What should you ask the program before you agree?

Ask five things, and get the answers in writing:

1. Which token, and on which network?
2. The exact mint or contract address it will send.
3. Who pays the network fee, and who creates the token account?
4. The exchange rate date, if your commission is set in another currency.
5. What happens if a payment goes to the wrong network.

Then send yourself a small test payment and cash it out before you rely on the route.

## FAQ

**Is USDC safer than USDT?**
This article cannot answer that, and the issuers' pages do not either. They show differences you can check: what each says backs the token, how reserves are reported, and who may redeem.

**Can I swap one for the other after I am paid?**
Usually, on an exchange or in a wallet that supports both on the same network. You pay that service's fee and take its rate, so being paid in the token you can cash out directly saves a step.

**Why did I receive the tokens but cannot see them?**
On Solana each token sits in its own token account. Check that your wallet shows the account for the correct mint address, and that the payment was sent on Solana.

**Does the 1:1 peg mean I always get one dollar?**
The issuers state 1:1 redemption for their own verified customers. You will normally sell through a third party, at its rate and after its fees.

**Should my commission be quoted in the token or in my currency?**
Agree it in writing. If the commission is set in another currency, the date and source of the exchange rate decide how many tokens you receive.
